One loop drives every beat

The Nightmare wants something ordinary. The world offers a plausible obstacle. The player looks, takes, uses and combines until it gives way, and the dream cracks a little further. Fail, and the day rolls over: the house resets, but what the player learned stays.

flowchart LR
  W["Wake"] --> Want["Want something ordinary"]
  Want --> Obs["Obstacle at the current tier"]
  Obs --> Act["Look, Take, Use, Combine"]
  Act -->|solved| Crack["The dream cracks: tier rises"]
  Crack --> Want
  Act -->|failed| Roll["Day rolls over"]
  Roll -->|"house resets, knowledge stays"| W

Tiers are Plausible, Suspicious and Open. Payoffs (T9, L7) are full-colour release valves between them.

Look, Take, Use, Combine

Verbs

  • Look always works and prints one sincere line of narration in Cormorant italic. Lines change with state and with knowledge from earlier days.
  • Take moves a pickup into one of four slots. A full inventory refuses politely.
  • Use applies the selected item to the target, or the bare hand when nothing is selected. Wrong items get a specific line, never a generic "can't do that".
  • Combine exists in the system now but is taught in Find a Job (the CV beat), so the tutorial stays three verbs.

Input and feedback

  • One interact key (E, gamepad face bottom) uses the context verb shown in the prompt; a second key cycles Look and Use when both apply.
  • Prompt reads like [E] Use the screwdriver on the lock plate: a full sentence in IBM Plex Sans, no separators.
  • Focus highlight is a soft rim from the dream post-process on custom stencil 3. Stencil 1 already keeps colour and 2 is the character's tear glitch.
  • Interactions play a montage from the animation lane and are spatially anchored: the Nightmare kneels at the boot and at the lock.

Getting dressed, in numbers

Turn-based and mechanically simple. Every move is a mundane verb with a sincere name and real numbers, all tunable in DT_Moves. A typical win takes six to eight turns.

The Nightmare

  • Button Up, 10 Resolve. A three-key timed input; each hit fastens one button, a perfect run fastens two. Blocked while the armhole is hidden.

The move pattern is weighted by tier and by attempt: on a later day the Shirt opens with the move the player already knows, so knowledge shortens the fight.

The same BP_Encounter runs Find a Job's L2, where the CV is the opponent and Combine turns the morning into experience: "Expert in lock removal."

The day rolls over, knowledge stays

On a fail the black-sand mare gallops through the room, the phone adds a crossed-out day and every BPI_Interactable resets to its morning state. GI_FoA keeps the flags below, which change Look lines, phone notebook entries and shortcuts.

The dream director

A hidden tier picks which variant of each obstacle plays, so escalation follows progress and attempt count without hand-scripting every combination. BP_BeatDirector decides; BP_DreamDirector shows it.

Plausible

Grey world at rest. Light echoes on fast camera moves only. Obstacles are ordinary bad luck.

Suspicious

Echoes on the Nightmare, rare tear glitch, furniture moves when unobserved, hue drifts one step.

Open

The dream fights back: encounters, sand bursts, heavy echoes. Each pass raises the base tier of the next obstacle.

Lean budget

16mechanics assets for both levels
5interactable actor classes
3data tables carry every item, beat and move
0per-puzzle Blueprint classes
  • Props in the room become interactables by swapping their actor to the right child class; meshes and materials are reused as they are.
  • Graphs follow the house style: titled comment boxes with zoom bubbles, left-to-right flow, no overlapping nodes, timers instead of Tick.
  • Anything superseded during the build is deleted once nothing references it.

Decisions for you

A second way to fail in the tutorial?

Right now only the shirt can roll the day over. Option: the fear-of-adulthood clock on the commode rings at 09:00 game time (about twelve real minutes) and rolls the day. It adds pressure and makes the loop visible earlier.

Which four Find a Job beats?

The recommendation is L2, L4, L6 and L7 because they reuse the encounter, the top-down camera and the payoff. Swap any of them for L1, L3 or L5 if a beat matters more than the build cost.

Encounter difficulty

The numbers above aim at a first-try win for most players. Tougher means a likely first loss, which shows off the rollover at the cost of a repeat.
