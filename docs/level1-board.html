The rule of the dream

Funny things, filmed sincerely.

In a dream, absurd things feel deadly serious. That's the effect we want. The camera never winks, the music never plays a joke sting, and the protagonist reacts with quiet disbelief, then keeps going. The comedy lands because nothing in the world admits it's funny.

Serious camera

Third person, close over the shoulder and never in a hurry, lit only by lamps and the bay window. Top-down only for T7, L4 and L6, where seeing the layout is the joke.

Plausible first

Every obstacle starts as ordinary bad luck. Dream logic creeps in only after the player has accepted the world as real.

Heroic mundane

Small wins get staged like action-movie climaxes: a low angle, full color, black sand bursting. Three seconds later, the world is grey again.

Reference · Twelve Minutes (2021)

One small home and a loop where the only upgrade is what you know. This is our puzzle rhythm, and its overhead camera becomes our occasional top-down shot.

Reference · Coraline (Laika, 2009)

The Other World is a comfortable trap that wants you to stay, full of faces that are almost right. So is the Sleeper's dream.

Reference · Fuseli, The Nightmare (1781)

A sleeper, a weight on her chest, and a horse's head pushing through the curtain. That's our night mare: your black-sand horse belongs here, and frame F15 boards it.

Black sand

Black everywhere, mounds included. It moves like smoke and settles like sand. Lux3D Gaussian splats drawn as splat meshes, with no Niagara.

Time-shift blur

Used a lot. When time slips, the Nightmare leaves multiple-exposure echoes of itself, faded copies of the poses it just left.

Tear glitch

A few times only, a horizontal tear runs through the Nightmare and shifts it in bands, then it snaps back.

Light shafts

Additive cards through the bay window. The material is T_Lines band texture × gradient × depth fade × camera depth fade × fresnel.

The bedroom

A 7.5 × 6 m antique bedroom with a three-sided bay window, a chimney breast with mantel and firebox, and full trims. Free CC0 furniture, Lux3D dream objects.

Hue drift. The monochrome is split-toned: shadows and highlights carry faint, different tints. The hue drifts slightly as the story progresses, starting as a cold blue-grey night in the tutorial. One shared parameter set drives it, so every level stays in step.

Camera. Gameplay is third person over the shoulder, on Unreal's Third Person template. Level 1 · Find a job

Goal: write a CV and reach the employment office. The world opens up, and the coincidences get harder to explain.

Mechanics

One loop drives every beat. The systems underneath stay small enough to build in thirteen days.

Interact Doc

Look, Take, Use, Combine. A small inventory (four slots is the idea). The items are ordinary. Their consequences aren't.

Time loop Doc

Failing rolls the day over. The house resets, and the player's knowledge carries over. Idea: a notebook on the phone that records facts you've discovered.

Dream director Idea

A hidden tier value (Plausible, Suspicious, Open) picks which variant of each obstacle plays. Escalation then tracks the player's progress and attempt count without hand-scripting every combination.

Encounter sketch · T8 Getting dressed

Turn-based and mechanically simple. Every move is a mundane verb with a sincere name and real numbers. Stats are Composure (HP) and Resolve (MP), an idea; frame F08 mocks them up as HP and Focus. The doc's own example: in Level 3, staring into the bathroom mirror restores 10 HP.

Hero asset pipelines

Everything looks real. Anything the player touches is a mesh, and smoke and sand are Lux3D splats drawn as splat meshes. Lux3D makes the dream objects, the sand and some set dressing; the furniture is free CC0.

G1Image or text in. ZIP + GLB with PBR out, plus a PLY splat when requested. Mesh in about 5 min.
G1-TurboCan return a PLY splat on its own. A splat preview takes about 20 s, which makes it our iteration loop.
Retopology · part splitRetopologizes to a face budget and splits a GLB into parts (drawers, doors, key halves).
Articulation · auto-rigAdds joints and animation to a GLB (hinges, lids). Character auto-rig plus animation retargeting.

Smoke and sand

The signature FX: sand hands, trails, waves, the mare. No Niagara.

Splat mesh
  1. Reference imagehand, horse, any object
  2. Lux3D G1-TurboPLY Gaussian splat
  3. PLY → splat meshone quad per Gaussian, in one static mesh
  4. Materialcheap effects move it

Each quad keeps its Gaussian's spherical harmonics, so its color changes with the view. Any Lux3D object can become a splat mesh, so set dressing can crumble into sand on cue.

Budget: under 1 GB, on low-end PCs

Procedural, cheap materials everywhere. The biggest risk is the splat meshes. Every Gaussian becomes a quad, so quad count and overdraw set the cost, and quads inside one static mesh aren't depth-sorted each frame, so blending can show order artifacts. Spike S2 measures both on a low-end PC and sets a quad cap per splat.

  1. S1 · Mesh round tripLux3D image → GLB → retopo → imported into Unreal at the correct scale with working PBR.
  2. S2 · Splat meshOne Lux3D PLY becomes one static mesh of quads with spherical-harmonic color. A material raises a hand from a mound and pours it away.
  3. S3 · Low-end budgetThe bedroom with sand, smoke and light shafts on a low-end PC: frame time, and a build under 1 GB.
  4. S4 · Garments on the mannequinLux3D hood, harness and mask skinned to the UE mannequin, walking across the bedroom on the Third Person animations.

To decide together

  1. Payoff palette. Frames E and F14 put a white suit under a cobalt and saffron sky, with a red stamp. Board v1 had a cobalt suit with magenta lapels. Do we lock the frames' version?
  2. Where else does the black-sand mare appear? F15 boards it as the day rollover. Also on the main menu, or as the Nightmare's true form? Its gallop has to work with material effects alone.
  3. Hue drift. The tutorial starts as a cold blue-grey night. Which way does the split tone drift by the end of Level 1, and how far?
  4. Where the tear lands. The tear glitch hits the Nightmare only a few times. Which beats get it? T7, L4 and the fail loop are candidates.
  5. The street. Modular meshes with Lux3D dressing, splat-mesh facades in the distance, or both?
  6. Level 1 scope. Which 3–4 of the seven beats make the cut? Four beats still need frames: T3, T10, L3 and L4.
  7. Do we ever see the Sleeper? Coraline has the Other Mother as its comfortable trap. Who or what is ours?
  8. Lanes. Who takes gameplay and who takes art and FX?
